Rich black vs plain black

Your big black background printed as a flat, slightly grey black instead of a deep solid. That's plain black — 100% K alone. For large solid areas you want a rich black, built from black plus a little of the other inks.

Why plain black looks thin

A single layer of black ink isn't fully opaque — some paper shows through, so a large area of 100% K reads as a washed-out, almost charcoal black. It can also look uneven across a big fill. Fine for text; disappointing for a full-bleed black background.

What rich black is

Rich black lays black ink plus a base of cyan, magenta, and/or yellow underneath, so the area reads as a deep, even, true black. A common build:

Rich black: around C40 M30 Y30 K100. Keep total ink coverage near 300%. Many presses have their own preferred recipe — ask your printer, since it depends on the stock and press.

Don't overdo it — the ink limit

More ink isn't always better. If you stack 100% of all four inks (that's 400% coverage), the paper can't absorb or dry it — it stays wet, smears, offsets onto the next sheet, and can crack. That's why rich black stays around 300%, not 400%. This cap is called the total area coverage (TAC) limit.

When to use plain black instead

Use plain K100 for small text and thin lines. Rich black uses four inks that have to register (line up) perfectly; on small type, even a hair of misregistration shows as a colored fringe around the letters. So: rich black for big solid areas, plain black for text.

FAQ

Why does my black look grey / faded?
Almost always because it's 100% K alone over a large area. Rebuild those big black fills as a rich black (K100 plus a C/M/Y base) and they'll read deep and even.
What's the max ink coverage?
Most presses cap total ink coverage around 300% (coated stock) and lower for uncoated. Going near 400% causes smearing and drying problems, which is why rich black isn't just "100% of everything."
Does this matter for digital printing?
Less than offset, but still yes — large K-only areas can look flat on digital too. When it matters, use a modest rich black and test.
