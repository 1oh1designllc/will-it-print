How to make a print-ready PDF

"Print-ready" isn't a vague stamp of approval — it's a specific checklist. A print-ready PDF has the right size, bleed, resolution, color, and fonts, exported so a press can run it without a single question. Here's the whole thing.

The six things a press needs

  1. Correct trim size — built at the exact finished dimensions, right orientation.
  2. Bleed — artwork extended 0.125 in past the trim on every edge, so the cut never leaves a white sliver. More on bleed →
  3. Resolution — raster images at about 300 DPI at final size, so nothing prints soft. More on 300 DPI →
  4. CMYK color — not RGB, so colors don't shift on press. More on RGB vs CMYK →
  5. Embedded fonts — so your text can't be substituted. More on embedding fonts →
  6. Exported as PDF/X with crop marks — the print-standard format, with trim marks telling the press where to cut.

Why PDF (not JPG or PNG)

A PDF can hold vector artwork, live embedded fonts, a defined trim and bleed, and CMYK color — all in one file. A JPG or PNG is a flat raster image with none of that, which is why they so often print soft or wrong. For print, PDF is the format, and PDF/X is the professional flavor of it.

Export settings (any app)

When you export, aim for:

  • Preset: PDF/X-1a (safest, flattened) or PDF/X-4 (keeps transparency live).
  • Marks & Bleeds: turn on crop marks and "use document bleed settings."
  • Color: convert to CMYK (your printer's profile if they gave you one).
  • Fonts: embedded (PDF/X does this automatically).
Quick per-app notes: InDesign/Illustrator — File → Export/Save As → Adobe PDF (Print) → pick a PDF/X preset. Canva — Download → "PDF Print" → check "Crop marks and bleed." Word/Publisher — these aren't print tools; embed fonts, export PDF, and expect to add bleed manually (or rebuild in a real layout app for anything important).

FAQ

Can I just use a regular PDF?
Often yes, if it already has the right size, bleed, CMYK color, and embedded fonts. PDF/X just enforces those automatically so you don't miss one. When in doubt, check it.
What size should my PDF be?
The finished (trim) size plus bleed. A 3.5×2 in card becomes a 3.75×2.25 in PDF with crop marks showing the 3.5×2 cut. The printer trims it down.
My printer gave me a color profile — what do I do with it?
Load it as the destination profile in your PDF export's color settings. It tells your file how that specific press reproduces color, so your proof matches the print more closely.
